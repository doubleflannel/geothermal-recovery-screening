# Project One-Pager

## What This Is

This is a preliminary public-data research packet for geothermal thermal-recovery screening. The aim is to test whether public temperature-recovery records can be reduced to clear recovery descriptors and, when the metadata are strong enough, mapped to bulk/model-equivalent permeability estimates in `m²`.

The packet is intentionally small. It shows one worked EGS Collab example, one Utah FORGE metadata request rationale, and a plain evidence-discipline page that explains what is fit-ready versus still blocked.

## Why It Matters

Public geothermal datasets often contain excellent measurements, but the calculation can fail if the surrounding metadata are incomplete. For permeability-oriented thermal recovery, the important missing pieces are usually clocks, units, channel definitions, pressure, flow, inlet or production temperature, depth references, and operation windows.

This packet treats those gaps as part of the result. A dataset can be promising without being fit-ready. The public claim should say that clearly instead of pretending the missing metadata do not matter.

## Current Public Evidence

- EGS Collab Exp2 AMU 34 m is included as a worked public-data example. It shows a temperature recovery descriptor, a pressure/flow boundary calculation, and a same-depth-scale permeability comparison in `m²`.
- Utah FORGE August 2025 16B is included as a source-request-needed candidate. The public DTS package and paper/report context are promising, but the row-level Liberty/Pason pressure-flow-temperature forcing export for Aug. 26–30, 2025 has not been located in the inspected public package.
- The evidence-discipline page explains the labels used here: `fit-ready`, `partial-adjacent-found`, `source-request-needed`, and `model-bridge-blocked`.

## What This Does Not Claim

This packet does not claim that the full method is validated across many datasets. It does not claim that Utah FORGE August 2025 permeability has been estimated. It also does not claim that model-equivalent permeability is the same as measured fracture permeability.

The useful claim is narrower: public geothermal data can be screened responsibly when the evidence ladder is visible and the missing metadata are named.
